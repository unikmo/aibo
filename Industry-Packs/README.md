# Industry packs

Industry packs make AIBO genuinely useful in a particular type of business. They add operational knowledge to the shared AIBO core; they do not replace it.

Each pack contains the customer journey, the measures that matter, useful working templates, the systems normally involved, and the decisions that need owner approval. A project uses only the pack or add-on that matches its business.

## Local Services families

Some local businesses share the Local Services operating model but need a different family-level workflow before a specialist add-on can apply.

- `Local-Services/Families/Mobile-Auto` — vehicle, roadside, parts and property-safety workflow; initial modules: Mobile Mechanic and Detailing.
- `Local-Services/Families/Beauty-Wellness` — appointments, consent, hygiene, privacy and client-care workflow; initial modules: Hair and Massage.
- `Local-Services/Families/Pet-Care` — animal welfare, owner authority, care records and handover workflow; initial modules: Grooming and Dog Walking.
- `Local-Services/Families/Tutoring` — learner outcomes, guardian contact, safeguarding and education-record workflow; initial modules: Academic Subjects and Languages.
- `Local-Services/Families/Health-Medical-Services` — US health-service operations and HIPAA-aware boundaries; initial modules: Dentistry, Physician Practices, ABA Providers, Mental Health, Rehabilitation and Pharmacies.

Use one family when it applies. A family may later have several specialist add-ons.

## How a pack is used

1. Soni confirms the project’s industry and location.
2. AIBO reads the shared core, then the relevant pack and any specialism.
3. The pack informs research, planning, delivery, review, measurement and approval checks.
4. The project keeps its own business facts, working files, approvals and results. A pack never overwrites them.

## Pack standard

A new pack must provide practical, industry-specific operating guidance. Marketing phrases alone are not a pack. It should define:

- the journey from first enquiry to repeat business;
- the language, evidence and metrics used in that industry;
- the key templates and service playbooks;
- the systems commonly connected and what they are allowed to do;
- financial, safety, legal and reputational checks; and
- which decisions need explicit owner approval.
